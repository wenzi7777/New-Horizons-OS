# v2.1.0

**Apps can now be written as imperative programs (TypeScript style), with new system calls: storage, messages between apps, device settings and streaming, and external I2C / UART on v1.5.F / v2.0.F. Dangerous capabilities need the operator's consent at install.**

## App system calls

- **Storage** (`storage`): up to 16 numbers per app (`store.get` / `store.set`, kept in NVS, written at most every 30 s and when the app stops) and CSV files (`store.append`, written to `<id>/<file>.csv` in the appdata scope). An app's files share a 16 KB quota; a row past it is refused and the app gets one `store_full` event, it is not stopped. Rows go through a fixed 256 B buffer per app and reach flash when it is full or every 10 s.
- **Messages between apps** (`app_ipc`): `app.publish(topic, value)` reaches every other app subscribed with `on("message", topic, …)`; each app queues 4, and overflow counts in `events_dropped`.
- **Device settings** (`device_config`): while it runs, an app may change `scan.target_fps`, `scan.send_every_n_frames`, `filter.enabled/median/alpha` and `imu.enabled`, within the usual ranges, at most once a second, and **never saved to NVS**; the operator's value comes back when the app stops, faults or is removed.
- **Streaming** (`device_stream`): `device.stream(false/true)` pauses and resumes the stream to the relay; it resumes by itself when the app stops.
- **External I2C** (`io_i2c`, v1.5.F / v2.0.F only): 7-bit addresses, the board's own chips (0x14, 0x36, 0x3C/0x3D, 0x41, 0x60, 0x68, 0x6A) always refused; at most 32 B per transfer, 5 ms timeout, a NACK returns -1 instead of stopping the app, and the transfer time counts against the app's slice.
- **External UART** (`io_uart`, v1.5.F / v2.0.F only): FPC1.17 TX / FPC1.16 RX, 1200–921600 baud; the driver and its 256 B receive buffer exist only while open, and stopping the app closes it and returns the pins to inputs.
- Array arguments are range-checked inside the call; out of range is a `bad_syscall_arg` fault. Still no heap allocation while an app runs.

## Apps: no fixed number, one resource pool

- The four fixed app slots are gone. Apps share one 8 KB pool and the per-frame time budget, and as many run as fit: one heavy app can take almost all of it, or many light ones can run together. A full pool answers `app_pool_exhausted`; loop-free apps whose estimates exceed the time budget answer `app_budget_exhausted`. Up to 16 packages can be installed (the index; flash is the real limit).
- Turning an app on (`app_activate`) starts it now and at every boot; `app_deactivate` stops it and frees its memory. Apps are named by their id everywhere (`app_list`, `app_view`, `app_events`, `/proc/apps`, the events CSV): no more `flow1`. A `slot` argument is accepted and ignored.
- Memory an app needs is fixed at build time and taken in full when it starts, so a running app never runs out of memory. Storage buffers are allocated only for apps with the `storage` capability.
- Each running app reports its load: CPU over 1 s / 10 s / 60 s and a 0–100 `load` with a level (`low` / `medium` / `high` / `overloaded`), in `app_list`, `app_view` and `/proc/apps`.
- Time is shared fairly, capped at half the budget per app (all of it when only one runs). An app built with `burst: true` may borrow time other apps are not using, recomputed every second and taken back as soon as they need it; never guaranteed. `app_list` reports `base_us`, `borrowed_us` and `lent_us`.
- New `app_reorder` (`id`, `to`): the earlier app wins shared OLED rows and the status LED.
- Removed: `app_enable` / `app_disable` (use `app_activate` / `app_deactivate`, or `app_stop` / `app_start` to pause until reboot) and the old debug path `app_load_flow` / `app_unload_flow` / `apps/flow.json`.
- An index from v2.0.x is migrated: an app that had a slot stays on, in slot order.
- Static RAM is about 5 KB higher than v2.0.0 (the 8 KB pool replaces the 4 KB one and the four app objects; the package index holds 16 entries instead of 8), so about 6 KB less heap is free (110 KB on a v2.0.F with four apps).

## Capabilities and consent

- The capability mask is now 32 bits, adding `storage`, `app_ipc`, `device_config`, `device_stream`, `io_i2c` and `io_uart`.
- `app_install` takes a new `grant` (array of strings). A package declaring a dangerous capability (storage, device_config, device_stream, io_i2c, io_uart) that was not granted is refused (`capability_not_granted:<cap>`), as is one asking for I/O the board does not have (`capability_unsupported:<cap>`). Grants are kept in the package index and reported by `app_list_packages` as `granted`.
- Removing an app also removes its values and appdata files.

## Compatibility

- App Library 3.0.0 apps are compiled from TypeScript-style source and need firmware v2.1.0; 2.0.0 apps keep running on v2.1.0.
- Installing 3.0.0 apps needs New Horizons Desktop v1.12.0 / NH Studio v2.8.0 or later (older versions cannot validate a package without `nodes` and never send `grant`).
